A wedding invitation announces the celebration. A useful guest-information sheet helps people arrive at the right place, understand which parts they are joining and know whom to contact. For a Phuket wedding with guests travelling from different places, a short set of confirmed details can prevent a long chain of repeated messages.

Royal Phuket City Hotel offers wedding enquiries in Phuket Town. This guide explains how to prepare information for your guests once you have discussed the arrangements for your own celebration. It complements the host's working programme rather than replacing it.

Put the essential arrival details first

Begin with the couple's names, the full event date, the venue and the time guests should arrive. Make the date unambiguous for people accustomed to different written formats. If you include a time, identify it as local Phuket time and distinguish the guest arrival time from the start of a ceremony or meal.

The hotel address is 154 Phang-Nga Road, Talad Yai, Muang, Phuket 83000, Thailand. Confirm the exact arrival point and the route to your event space with the wedding team. The hotel address gets people to the property; it does not by itself tell them where to gather for your particular celebration.

Add a map link you have checked, a clear meeting instruction and one contact for a guest who cannot find the group. Avoid circulating an old map screenshot with an uncertain pin. Ask someone unfamiliar with the venue to read the directions before you send them.

Show which parts each guest is invited to

A ceremony, reception and later gathering may have different guest lists or locations. List the parts that apply to each invitation clearly. Do not leave a guest to infer whether an afternoon family activity or an optional evening gathering is included.

Where relatives have a role in a family ceremony, send them the relevant instructions separately after checking them with the people organising that part. Explain when they should be ready and whom to speak to, without putting the entire internal running order on every guest's sheet.

For example, one version might contain the reception details only, while another also includes a confirmed family ceremony meeting point. That is a communication method, not a prescribed wedding format. The hosts decide the invitation scope and confirm the practical arrangements with the venue.

Keep accommodation details separate from the RSVP

A guest's reply that they will attend does not reserve a hotel room. If people will stay overnight, explain how they should enquire or book, which stay dates they need to consider and who handles accommodation questions. Share a room arrangement only after the hotel has confirmed the details that apply.

Link to rooms and suites for the hotel's room information. If you are discussing a group arrangement, ask what guests should quote, how they should contact reservations and which conditions must be communicated. Do not describe an unconfirmed rate, included breakfast or transfer as part of the invitation.

Collect requests through one reply channel

State how guests should reply and the deadline chosen for your celebration. Ask the venue which information it needs and when it needs it, then set your guest-response plan accordingly. Use one organiser to collect changes so a revised headcount does not disappear among several conversations.

Invite guests to raise meal, seating or access requirements through that contact. Record the request, discuss it with the relevant team and reply with the arrangement that has actually been confirmed. A submitted request should remain visibly pending until you have an answer.

Keep individual requirements in the organiser's working notes rather than in a sheet shared with every guest. The public-facing information can explain the contact route; the detailed conversation can happen separately with the guest and venue.

Explain dress and practical expectations plainly

If you have chosen a dress instruction, write it in everyday language and explain any detail guests might otherwise misunderstand. Ask the venue and ceremony organisers about requirements relevant to your event before sending the message. Avoid assuming that a broad cultural or ceremony label provides every guest with the same instructions.

Tell guests about any confirmed movement between spaces and any practical arrangements they need to make themselves. If you plan an organised journey, state its meeting point, departure time and booking responsibility only after those details are agreed. Otherwise, explain that guests should arrange their own journey and allow time for it.

You can also share the hosts' wishes about photographs, announcements or a particular family moment. Keep the instruction brief and courteous. A few clear requests are easier to follow than a long list of implied rules.

Give updates one home and one owner

Choose one place for the current guest details and one person responsible for updating it. When something changes, send a short message identifying the changed detail and where to find the current version. Update the original information sheet as well as the conversation, so the older instruction does not remain the easiest one to find.

Keep supplier cues and backstage responsibilities in the separate reception running order. Guests usually need the arrival point, their invitation scope and a contact; they do not need every catering cue or equipment discussion.

Read the sheet as a guest before sending it

Ask a friend to answer five questions from the sheet alone: which date, where to arrive, what time, which parts to attend and whom to contact? Then check that every statement about rooms, transport or individual arrangements matches the confirmation you received.
