A useful corporate event venue brief tells the venue what the event must achieve, who is attending, how the programme works and which requirements are essential. Include your preferred dates, headcount range, room layout, equipment, catering, accommodation and decision process. A clear brief helps the venue prepare a relevant proposal instead of guessing from “meeting space for 100 people”.

The brief does not need to be polished design work. A short, well-organised document with honest uncertainties is more useful than a long presentation that omits the information needed to quote.

Begin with the purpose and audience

Explain what the event is meant to accomplish in one or two sentences. A distributor briefing, leadership meeting and hands-on product workshop may involve the same number of people but require different rooms and support.

Describe the audience in practical terms. Include the likely mix of local attendees and overnight visitors, whether guests arrive together and whether the session is internal or includes customers or media. Share access, language or dietary requirements where relevant, without circulating unnecessary personal information.

Separate the expected headcount from the maximum you might need. For example, an organiser expecting around 60 attendees but waiting for confirmation from another group should show both figures. Ask how that uncertainty affects the proposed room and quotation.

Give dates and the full operating window

Provide a preferred event date and any acceptable alternatives. Then describe when suppliers need access, when guests arrive, the programme window and when the room must be cleared. The event's public start time is only one part of the venue booking.

Explain whether rehearsals, deliveries or a setup visit are required. A presentation-led meeting may need a simple equipment check; a demonstration or stage production may need a longer technical session. Ask the venue to identify what is included and what needs a separate arrangement.

For a multi-day event, show each day's shape separately. Avoid describing several different programmes as one generic “full day”. The venue needs to understand when people eat, move between spaces and return for evening activities.

Describe the programme before requesting a room

Include an outline showing presentations, discussions, breaks and meals. It can be provisional. The important point is to explain what people do during each part of the event, so the room is chosen for the work rather than appearance alone.

Specify a preferred layout or ask for advice where you are undecided. Our meeting seating comparison explains the differences between theatre, classroom, U-shape and boardroom formats. Note whether attendees need laptops, printed documents or space for exercises.

If breakout sessions run at the same time, give the number of groups and the expected size of each. Mention whether separate sound, screens or facilitators are needed. An extra room on a floor plan is not automatically included in a main-room booking.

Turn “AV required” into a usable specification

List the presentations and equipment you actually need: speaker microphones, presentation screens, audio playback, connections for laptops and any remote participation. Explain who supplies the files and who operates the system.

For online attendance or recording, describe the intended outcome and ask the technical team to propose the setup. Do you need remote attendees to hear questions from the room? Will someone switch between presentation slides and a live demonstration? Those details affect staffing and equipment.

  • Number of speakers and the way they move around the room.
  • Presentation devices and connection requirements.
  • Video, music, live demonstrations or remote contributors.
  • Rehearsal requirements and responsibility for final files.
  • Technical support needed during the programme.

Request a written equipment schedule and identify any supplier-provided items. General website descriptions introduce capabilities; your proposal should confirm the equipment and support allocated to your event.

Include food, accommodation and arrival needs

State the meal occasions, approximate service windows and preferred style. A working lunch, seated dinner and short refreshment break require different planning. Provide the number of dietary requests when known and agree how final details will be collected securely.

If guests stay overnight, give the expected room numbers, stay pattern and whether bookings are centralised or made individually. Ask about the process for changes, payment and rooming lists. An event-space enquiry does not reserve guest rooms automatically.

Explain how guests are likely to arrive: coaches, individual cars or arranged transfers. Ask about unloading, registration positions and access for deliveries. If you have materials or products to ship, describe their size and handling needs rather than only saying “some boxes”.

Make the quotation easy to compare

Tell the venue whether you have a working budget and what it covers. If the figure includes accommodation, food, equipment and production, say so. Otherwise, a proposal for room hire alone may look cheaper while leaving much of your event uncosted.

Request separate lines for included services, optional extras and items still awaiting confirmation. Ask how taxes, service charges, overtime and changes are treated. This is about obtaining a clear commercial proposal, not assuming a standard policy across venues.

Name the person who can approve the booking and explain your decision deadline. Also ask how availability is held, what confirms the reservation and when final numbers are required. Keep proposed arrangements distinct from those already accepted in writing.

A concise brief you can adapt

We are planning a [type of event] in Phuket for [expected number] attendees, with a possible maximum of [number]. Our preferred date is [date], with [alternatives]. The programme includes [main activities], and we need access for [setup or rehearsal]. Please propose a suitable layout, itemised equipment, catering and any accommodation arrangements described below. Our priorities are [three essentials]. Please identify inclusions, optional charges, booking terms and the information needed to finalise the proposal.

Attach the programme and a short requirements list. Keep one current version and nominate a contact who can resolve questions. Repeated messages from different organisers can otherwise produce conflicting assumptions about the same booking.

Questions about event briefs

Can I enquire before every detail is confirmed?

Yes. Label estimates and unresolved choices clearly, and ask the venue what information is needed next. Uncertainty is manageable when it is visible.

Does sending a brief secure the venue?

No. Confirm the reservation process and commercial terms directly. An enquiry and an accepted booking are different stages.
