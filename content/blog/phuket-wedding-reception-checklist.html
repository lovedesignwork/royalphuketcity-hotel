Before choosing a Phuket wedding reception venue, visit with a clear event outline and a list of decisions to check. A beautiful room is a useful starting point, but your reception also depends on the layout, meal, guest journey and arrangements confirmed for your date. This checklist helps you turn a venue tour into a practical planning conversation.

Bring a short event outline to the visit

Start with your preferred date, an alternative date if possible, estimated guest numbers and the parts of the day you hope to host. Explain whether you are discussing a reception only or a ceremony and reception together. Include the approximate timing and who will make the final decisions.

Your first guest estimate can be a range. Ask when the venue needs a firmer number and how a later change would affect the proposed arrangement. This gives the team a useful brief without turning an early estimate into an assumption about capacity or availability.

List your three priorities before arriving. They might be keeping older relatives comfortable, allowing space for a particular programme or making the meal easy for guests. Bring reference images as a conversation aid, and distinguish a visual preference from an essential requirement.

Review the room with a proposed layout

Ask to see a layout for your estimated party rather than assessing an empty room alone. Look at the proposed table positions, stage or focal point, serving routes and space needed for the programme. A room can feel different once furniture, decorations and equipment are included.

Stand where guests would sit and ask about sightlines. Then follow the route between the entrance, seating and any planned activity. If you want to change the layout, ask the team to explain the practical effect before treating it as agreed.

Confirm the suitable guest number for the specific layout in writing. A general venue figure does not establish the number appropriate for your reception, and this guide does not give a capacity promise.

Follow the guest journey from arrival to departure

Walk through the arrival point, directions to the reception and the route guests will use when leaving. Ask how signage, greetings and the registration table could fit into that journey. A small decision about where people meet can make the opening of the celebration much clearer.

If a guest needs step-free access, a nearby seat or another arrangement, describe the requirement and ask the team to confirm the relevant route and facilities. Do not assume that a photograph establishes accessibility. Check the practical details with the people who will use them.

Discuss transport and parking for your own event. Ask what information should go on the invitation and whether there are any date-specific conditions. Give guests one confirmed address and arrival instruction rather than several competing meeting points.

Discuss the meal as part of the programme

Ask which catering arrangements can be proposed for your reception and how service would fit around speeches, photographs and other activities. The meal and the programme should support each other; a long gap or an unexpected serving change can affect the pace of the evening.

Collect essential meal requirements early and ask which can be accommodated. Confirm the response before communicating it to guests. For any tasting or menu discussion, ask how it is arranged and whether a charge applies rather than assuming it is included.

The hotel's dining overview introduces its restaurant options, but a wedding catering proposal is a separate arrangement. Restaurant photographs or menu descriptions should not be treated as the confirmed reception menu.

Separate confirmed inclusions from requested extras

Ask for a written list of what the proposal includes. Review the room arrangement, food and drinks, decor, equipment and staffing items relevant to your programme. Mark each requested addition clearly so it does not become an assumed inclusion.

Ask who supplies each item and who coordinates it on the day. If you intend to use an outside supplier, discuss access, setup and removal with the hotel before agreeing a schedule with that supplier. Confirm any conditions or charges through the venue's own proposal.

For cultural or family traditions, describe the planned sequence and the items involved. The team can discuss the arrangement you request; a photograph of another ceremony does not mean every detail is automatically available for your event.

Plan accommodation and responsibilities together

If relatives will stay overnight, review the rooms and suites separately from the reception proposal. Ask how room bookings will be handled, what information guests need and which dates are relevant. An event enquiry does not itself reserve accommodation.

Agree on one main contact for the couple or organising family, and one contact for the venue discussion. Keep a simple list showing who confirms guest numbers, meal requests, supplier details and the final programme. This prevents several people from giving different instructions about the same decision.

Use this checklist before leaving the venue

  • Confirm which date and spaces the proposal refers to.
  • Request a layout based on your estimated guest range and programme.
  • Check arrival directions and any individual access requirements.
  • Discuss catering, service timing and essential meal requests.
  • Separate inclusions, optional extras and outside supplier arrangements.
  • Ask about payment, changes and confirmation conditions in the written proposal.
  • Identify the next decision, responsible person and follow-up date.

Compare proposals using the same event brief. If one includes an item that another does not, ask for clarification before comparing the totals. Keep questions specific and request an updated written version when the agreed arrangement changes.

Quick answers for a wedding venue visit

Can I choose a venue from photographs alone?

Photographs help you shortlist a setting. A visit and a date-specific proposal help you understand the layout, guest journey and arrangements for your own reception.

Should the ceremony and reception be discussed together?

If both are part of your plan, describe the full sequence at the start. Ask the venue to explain the spaces, timing and setup arrangements it can propose.

What should I confirm after the visit?

Confirm the event brief, proposed arrangement, inclusions, charges and next steps in writing. Treat requests as requests until the venue confirms them.
