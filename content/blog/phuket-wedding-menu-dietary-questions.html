Choosing a Phuket wedding menu starts with the guests, the service style and the shape of the celebration. Before selecting dishes, find out which dietary needs must be discussed and how the caterer will turn the agreed menu into meals served to the right people at the right time.

A useful menu enquiry covers more than taste. It also makes space for ingredients, preparation, portion planning, children's meals and changes after the first quotation. Use these questions with your wedding coordinator and caterer before approving the final arrangements.

Collect clear dietary information from guests

Ask guests to describe their requirement in their own words. Keep food allergies, intolerances, religious requirements and preferences distinct so the catering team can ask the right follow-up questions. Avoid replacing a specific request with a broad label such as “special meal”.

Set a response date that leaves time for the caterer to assess requests before the final menu and numbers are due. Ask who will receive the information and how late changes should be submitted. Give one person responsibility for maintaining the current list.

Share personal information only with the people who need it to organise and serve the meal. A guest's name, requirement and table allocation may be useful for service; a public seating plan does not need to display the reason for their meal choice.

Choose a service style that fits the programme

Discuss whether shared dishes, plated courses or a buffet suit the guest numbers, room and timing. Ask how service will work around speeches, photographs and entertainment. A menu that reads well on paper still needs a realistic place in the running order.

With shared dishes, discuss how individual dietary meals will be identified and delivered. For a buffet, ask how guests can obtain ingredient information and how serving arrangements will be managed. For plated service, agree how staff will match each meal to the correct guest if someone changes seats.

Consider the pace for children and older relatives as well as the wedding party. Ask whether children's meals can be served at a different point and what that would require. Confirm these details in the service plan instead of relying on a general promise of flexibility.

Ask about ingredients and preparation together

For a food allergy, discuss the specific ingredient and the preparation process directly with the caterer. Ask about sauces, stocks, garnishes, shared equipment and serving utensils. A dish's name or appearance is not enough to establish whether it is suitable.

Food Standards Scotland's eating-out guidance recommends asking about ingredients, preparation and possible cross-contact. Use those practical questions in the catering conversation; they do not establish what a particular Phuket venue can provide.

Ask the caterer to explain any limitation clearly so the affected guest can discuss it before the event. Do not infer an allergy guarantee from a vegetarian option, or infer religious certification from a dish description. Where certification matters to your guests, request the relevant current documentation and scope.

Use a tasting to resolve specific choices

If a tasting is available, ask what it includes, who can attend, whether it carries a charge and how its selections relate to the proposed wedding menu. Bring the current dietary questions and a short list of choices you need to settle.

Look at the meal as a sequence. Consider the balance of dishes, the portion plan and the time needed for service. Ask which elements may change with availability and what the approval process is for a substitute. Photograph or record the agreed selections with permission so everyone refers to the same version.

Do not assume that a tasting dish or library photograph represents an included package item. Request a written menu naming the selected dishes and any agreed alternatives. Include cake, drinks and outside suppliers

Discuss the cake as part of the meal plan. Confirm who supplies it, delivery and storage arrangements, the serving time and whether cutting or plating carries a charge. Ask how ingredient information will reach the service team, particularly if an outside supplier makes the cake.

Apply the same clarity to drinks. Ask what is included, how quantities or service hours are measured and what happens when an allowance ends. If you plan to bring drinks or food, confirm the venue's policy and any charges before placing an order with another supplier.

Check whether supplier and crew meals are needed. Photographers, performers and production staff may have different working windows from guests. Agree numbers, timing and where those meals will be served so this detail does not become a last-minute request.

Approve one final menu and service sheet

Before the caterer's deadline, review the total guest count, children's meals, individual requirements, table references and service times together. Include the final dishes, agreed substitutions, drinks, cake arrangements and all quoted charges. Ask how changes after that point will be handled.

Nominate a contact who can answer service questions on the day. Give that person the final version and confirm who they should speak to at the venue. Ask the coordinator to check that kitchen and service teams are working from the same information.
